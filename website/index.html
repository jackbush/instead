A free browser extension that swaps time-hole websites for one thing to do instead. It isn’t a blocker, just a few seconds to consider something that might make you feel better.

Add to Chrome

You already know what feels good.

It’s probably a walk, or a chat with a friend, or making something with your hands. It isn’t the feed, which is designed to eat your time and leave you less satisfied with what you have, so you buy more and they can sell more ads.

  1. Open a site on your list.

    Facebook, Instagram, Reddit, TikTok, YouTube, Twitter/X and LinkedIn to begin with, or any site you add. Your browser lands on Instead before the site loads.

  2. Get one suggestion instead.

    Something fun, free and fulfilling, big enough to read from across the room. If you don’t fancy it, ask for another.

  3. Do it, or go anyway.

    “I’m on it” closes the tab, and off you go. Or wait five seconds and carry on to the site.

The feed is built like a slot machine.

Instagram, Tinder, Facebook and YouTube borrow heavily from casinos. One trick is intermittent reward, where you get a mix of things you like and things you don’t, so you keep scrolling for another hit. Another is rewarding you with likes for posting the content that draws everyone else further in. Thousands of smart people are paid to make this work on you.

Blockers say no. Instead says what else.

There’s no wall, because a wall just makes you want to climb it. After five seconds, “Take me to …” lets that tab through for 15 minutes. Nothing is locked and there are no streaks.

Make it work for you.

  • Choose the sites you want to use less.
  • Let your direct messages through.
  • Make exceptions, like a single subreddit or group.
  • Pick the kinds of suggestion you like, and add your own.

A new sky every time.

The colours change on every visit and with every new suggestion, so the screen stays fresh. Feeds do something similar, but this isn’t to sell you anything.

Questions

Why not just block the sites?

A blocker leaves you with nothing to do, so you find a way round it or turn it off. A short pause and something else to do is usually enough.

Isn’t this just a willpower problem?

No. Too many people will tell you to learn control and restraint, which ignores the thousands of smart people whose job is to keep you scrolling and spending.

Why would a few seconds make a difference?

We all know these products don’t actually make us feel good, and often only a tiny break is needed to break the pattern. A few seconds is enough to consider doing something that might make you feel better.

Can I get past it?

Yes, on purpose. Wait five seconds, click “Take me to …”, and that tab is let through for 15 minutes.

What can it see?

Only that you opened a site on your list. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Does it work on my phone?

Not yet. It’s Chrome on a computer for now, with Firefox coming soon.

What about my data?

Instead runs entirely in your browser and only asks for access to the sites on your list. There’s no account and no analytics. The code is open source (MIT). Read the privacy policy

What does it cost?

Nothing. There’s no paid version and no ads.

Where can I read more?

The Center for Humane Technology, Stolen Focus by Johann Hari and Scarcity Brain by Michael Easter.
